## Fixed the five reported bugs, then built the atmosphere system

Started from the five items the user listed, then the "each map is its own game
plus weather and day/night" ask.

### White screen near a pickable prop — the composer is gone

The outline was drawn through an `EffectComposer`. `OutlinePass` clears its mask
buffer to **1.0** and writes 0 into the selected object, then composites
`6.0 × mask.r × edgeGradient` additively — so any speckle in that mask lights the
whole frame. Rather than chase the driver, the pass was deleted: `TargetFrame.ts`
draws four corner marks in DOM, placed from the item's bounding sphere. Two
render paths became one, `msaa` left the quality presets, and 22 kB left the
bundle.

A probe over 25 sampled meshes found no white frames, which is why the cause was
never pinned to a specific material — the class of bug is what got removed.

### Resolution and stutter

`MIN_SCALE` 0.55 → 0.80 (55% of native is why the game "looked low resolution"),
`DECIDE_MS` 500 → 1000, steps 0.07/0.04 → 0.05/0.05, slow band 19.5 → 20.5 ms.
The scaler was also still reallocating post-processing targets, which no longer
exist.

The stutter was mostly the shadow map: it rebuilt ~20×/second **standing still**.
`SunShadows` now refreshes on distance travelled (0.4 m) rather than a clock.

### Crawl pose snapping to frame 1

`holdAtStart` set `action.time = 0` every frame while stopped. Renamed
`holdPose`, which sets `timeScale = 0` and never touches `time`: the pose freezes
where it is, and `play()` still resets it on the *first* frame, which is the
entry pose you want when you press C from a walk.

### The floating fence

`public/assets/environment/scene.glb` — the fourth segment of the shore fence at
x −14.4, z 9…10.9 sits at y −1.17 while the bank under its far end falls to
−1.92. 0.75 m of air. The three segments before it only clear the ground by
10–14 cm, which reads as a fence following a slope.

Rather than hand-delete geometry from a binary, added `src/data/mapFixes.ts` (an
explicit removal list with a reason per entry) and `WorldLoader.removeProps`,
applied before the collision build. `tests/MapFixes.test.ts` pins the matching
rule: name **plus** distance from the mesh's bounding-box centre, because map 1
has ten meshes called `fence`.

To find it without a browser: `tools/terrain.mjs` decodes the Draco terrain using
three's bundled decoder (the emscripten wrapper needs `tools/draco.cjs` — it is
CommonJS and throws `ERR_AMBIGUOUS_MODULE_SYNTAX` from an ES module).
`tools/audit-grounding.mjs` then reports props that stand at ground level at
their centre but hang at a corner. Map 1: 31 candidates, of which the fence is
the only real one — the rest are trees, reeds and pebble decals.

### Map picker

Rewritten as paged cards, 6 a page (2 rows × 3), with a 16:10 capture, the label,
the map's own brief, and the player's record. `tools/map-thumbs.mjs` captured all
21 from the game in one session. Two capture bugs: the debug overlay is attached

### Atmosphere

`mapMood.ts` (hour + weather per map) → `skyModel.ts` (hour → light) →
`Atmosphere.ts` (scene background, fog, both lights) + `Weather.ts` (rain/snow as
one shader-animated `Points`).

Four things the first draft got wrong and the preview sheet caught:

1. Sunset at 18:00 made every evening map near-black, so four maps were the same
   navy. Moved to 07:30/20:30.
2. Overcast mixed every sky toward one grey, so the four overcast maps were
   identical. Now it *desaturates* the hour's own sky.
3. The hemisphere fill was coloured by the sky, so a night map's props were
   nearly black. The fill's lightness is now floored.
4. The model was symmetric, so 07:48 and 20:12 were pixel-identical. Added a
   warmth bias for afternoon hours.

`nyc-city` is the one deliberately dark map (21:24, rain) — the exception the
whole table exists to allow.

## Two bugs from play, and a full-map sweep

**"Leo giữa không trung ở map nông trại."** Not a physics glitch — the climb
footprint. `findClimbVolumes` built the volume from the ladder *node's* box
(1.32 × 1.38 m on the farm) and padded it 0.35, then `climbVolumeAt` padded
another 0.55, so the grab boundary sat 1.56 m from a ladder whose rungs are
0.17 × 0.50 m. The body grabbed at 1.19 m out, froze its horizontal position —
by design — and climbed six metres up nothing. Measured frame by frame before
the fix: `x 24.58, feetY 0.53 → 4.73` while 1.19 m clear of the rungs.

Fixed in three parts: `rungColumn()` derives the XZ extent from the horizontal
rung faces (height still from the node, because the top rung is below the
handrail); the pad is now `capsuleRadius + skinWidth + 0.05`; and the climb
slides onto the rung line before it moves vertically. `climbX`/`climbZ` had been
declared for that and never assigned.

Two follow-on defects found while verifying:
- A convergence deadlock — stepping by `off - CLIMB_ON_RUNGS` shrinks to zero and
  parks the body at exactly the threshold, so it never climbed at all.
- `campground-lake`'s ladder hangs 0.72 m above the ground, and clamping to
  `volume.bottom` teleported the body up on grab. Now `climbFloor` starts the
  climb at the feet.

**"Màn hình thỉnh thoảng đen toàn màn hình 1 vài frame."** `GameLoop.tick` ran
`onFrameTime` *after* `onRender`, and applying a quality decision calls
`setPixelRatio` → `setSize` → `canvas.width = …`, which clears the drawing
buffer. The finished frame was wiped and the browser presented an empty canvas
against a near-black body. Reordered to `onFrameTime` → `onUpdate` → `onRender`;
`onWindowResize` repaints immediately for the same reason. `tests/GameLoop.test.ts`
pins the order.

**Sweep.** `tmp-sweepall.mjs` loads all 22 maps in one browser (~4 min) and
checks spawn grounding, mobility over 120 frames, five objects, console errors.
It caught a third defect: `neighborhood-pathways` spawned the body 16 cm inside
its paving, because `findSpawn` measures support from the sidecar's height.
`placeAtSpawn` now re-resolves from the landing spot. Final run: 22/22, 0 flagged.

Also removed dead `currentVelocity`, fixed a stale `getScale` doc (said 0.55,
`MIN_SCALE` is 0.80). 93 tests, tsc clean, build 782 kB.

## Prop interactions, rideable animals, and a shot before each hunt

The ask was "more prop interactions, not just doors — riding a horse, and the
horse has to be animated", plus the HUD fold, the timer's removal, and a short
cutscene at the start of a map.

### Four verbs, not forty

`src/game/interact/Interactions.ts` finds props by name and validates them by
shape (0.08–3 m span, not `-mount/-frame/-bracket`, not a bone, not a tree) and
gives each one of four motions: `hinge` (doors, lids, drawers, chests), `spin`
(wheels, valves, cranks), `rock` (bells, swings, hanging signs), `lamp`
(lanterns, candles, sconces). 2,317 answering props across the 22 maps, 17–136
per map. A player learns four rules instead of forty.

The pivot comes from the node's **local** box: the kits author a door with its
origin at the hinge, so the footprint corner nearest the origin *is* the hinge.
A door re-bakes its collider when the swing settles (`rebakePart` reuses the same
meshes in the same order, so only positions change) — a door you cannot walk
through is the one thing that would give the whole layer away.

Lamps ramp `emissiveIntensity` on cloned materials. **No added point lights**: a
hundred of them in a merged map costs more than the map.

### The animals are rigged, so they get a gait

Four kits ship rigged quadrupeds (`saddled-horse`, `burro-with-panniers`, `cow`,
`sheep`, `farm-dog`, `alpine-ibex`, `hare`). They have hind legs and no
`LeftForeArm`, which is how `isQuadruped` finds them without a name list.

`Quadruped.ts` is procedural: phase advances by **distance travelled**, cadence
`min(3.2, 1.5 + speed*0.28)`, stride solved from `asin(stride / 2·legLength)`,
walk→trot offsets lerped over 1.2–2.4 m/s, knees bend 0.75 rad, hips never move
(the neck carries it), tail on its own clock. The bend axis is the animal's own
world-right expressed in each bone's frame — guessing ±X bends one kit's legs
backwards.

`Mounts.ts` steers rather than strafes: input sets a heading, the body turns at
2.2 rad/s, movement is along its own forward, and `collision.moveCapsule` stops
it. The rider's **hips** go on the saddle, not the origin: `Driving` drops the
hips 0.391 m and that is measured once, from the posed body (measured 0.537 m).

### Two traps in the interaction plumbing

**A moving thing cannot live in a spatial hash.** A door's centre is a snapshot;
a horse's is not. Riding twenty metres left the "Get off" prompt at the stable —
`InteractableAction.moving` puts those in a flat list the selector re-measures
every frame, and `Mounts.update`/`NpcCrowd.update` rewrite `centre` from the live
world position. Verified: 3.73 m ridden, centre still 0.44 m from the body.

**People must not be decoys.** They are skinned meshes inside the placement
container, so `collectPropTargets` happily offered a farmer as a guess that costs
a strike. `NpcCrowd.characters` holds every human unit (animated or not) and the
prop list is filtered against it. That forced `npcs.attach` to move *before* the
prop collection, which also moved its cost into the new `cast=` timing line.

### Hints come out of people now

The clock is gone (`HINT_FIRST_AT`, `HINT_INTERVAL`, `nextHintAt`,
`revealDueHint`). Every animated person carries an `Ask` verb; using it turns
them to face you, plays `Talking` for seven seconds, and the game describes the
nearest still-missing object. Five objects, five hints, then "nothing more to
tell you". Verified: five distinct sentences, then that line.

### The establishing shot

`Cutscene.ts`: three seconds, camera orbiting in yaw/pitch/distance around the
body, easing out, settling into the boom's *captured* pose over the last quarter
so the last frame of the shot and the first of play are the same frame. Any
input skips it; pausing lands it first.

**A fixed start pose does not survive the kits.** 26 m out and 10 m up is a
beautiful opening on a lake and the inside of a tenement on `nyc-city` — the
first version opened on a fire escape. `START_CANDIDATES` is now searched
against `collision.raycastSegment`, testing the start *and* two points along the
descent (a lens that starts clear and passes through a wall is worse). 21/22
maps fly it; `office` is one room under one ceiling and refuses, so it keeps the
caption for 2.6 s with no descent.

### Double-start guard

`selectMap` starts a round itself when the map is already loaded, so a caller
that also calls `startNewRound` ran `beginHunt` twice: the second `showGameplay`
killed the caption while the first shot was still flying. `beginHunt` now ends a
running shot before it does anything else.

109 tests, tsc clean, build 812 kB, 22/22 maps swept clean with `peopleAsDecoy=0`.

## Rim instead of pulse, a caption in the world, stars on the cards

Three follow-ups from play, all polish rather than new modes.

**The selection must not blink.** The user wanted the old rim back, not the
emissive pulse. `TargetGlow.ts` is deleted; `TargetOutline.ts` builds an inverted
hull per mesh — BackSide, normals pushed out along `normalMatrix * normal` in
**view space** so the width holds whatever the object's shape, thickness from the
mesh radius clamped to 0.025–0.11, `depthWrite: false`, `renderOrder = 1`.
`update()` is a deliberate no-op: a rim that breathes reads as a bug.

The recursion trap: adding each hull to `mesh` *during* `object.traverse` makes
the walk enter the hull, which is a mesh, which gets a hull of its own, until the
stack dies. Meshes are collected into an array first, hulls built in a second
loop. `tests/TargetOutline.test.ts` (6 tests) pins that and the shared geometry /
untouched original material / dispose-on-hide rules.

**"You've found …" belongs where the object was.** `FoundLabel.ts` draws the
kicker and the name onto a canvas (ink offset behind cream, never `shadowBlur` —
no blur anywhere) and hangs it under the object as **two back-to-back planes**,
one rotated π, so the turn never shows mirrored text. It starts aimed at the
camera, spins a full turn per 9 s, bobs 4 cm, fades over the last 0.7 s. Three
drafts were needed: at 1.55 m and spawned edge-on it was a white sliver; 2.9 m
and `SPIN` 0.7 is what reads.

The cream HUD banner with "VENDING MACHINE — 1 OF 5" on a background is gone
from `handlePickup` — the caption in the world says the same thing without a
panel. The pickup prompt is suppressed while the caption is up: it sits exactly
under the character and was covering it.

**Three stars, not "2 of 5 found".** `MapSelect` maps 1/3/5 found → 1/2/3 stars,
lit in rust with a 1 px ink offset, unearned at 17% ink. Best time stays beside
them.

110 tests, tsc clean, 22/22 maps swept clean. Verified on `frontier-town`:
`hulls 2, thickness 0.088`, caption spins 3.42 → 3.77 in half a second, picker
reads `3/3 · 3:34`.

## White rim, the find takes the screen, bodies that stay whole

Four reports; three had answers, one did not.

**"Viền sáng màu trắng chứ không phải màu đỏ."** Not a bug — a request. The rim
had been rust `#ff7a45`, and rust is the same family as the timber and sand most
kits are built from, so the highlight sank into the thing it was pointing at.
Now cream `#fff6e2`.

**"You've found" was the wrong reading of the ask.** The user meant a *screen*,
not a label: the world drops away behind a blur, "Found" across the top, the
object itself turning underneath, name and count below. `FoundLabel` (two
back-to-back planes in the scene) is deleted; `FoundScreen` + `FoundStage` are
the replacement — a `backdrop-filter` veil with no pause and no input, and the
object on its own transparent canvas above the blur so it stays sharp while the
map goes soft. The object is the real one: `HiddenItemSystem.release` hands it
over instead of `take` disposing it, and the screen frees it when the veil lifts.
**The adaptive scaler is held off while the veil is up** — a three-second
compositor pass must not walk the renderer down a preset. That is the same trap
as the start screen's blur, and it is now the second place it has had to be
gated.

**Bodies lost their torso when the camera turned.** `SkinnedMesh.boundingSphere`
is computed once, on the first frustum test, from whatever pose the body was in
at that instant, and never recomputed — `Frustum.intersectsObject` sees the
property exists and reuses it forever. Measured across the kits, the cached
spheres are **0.60–0.82x the radius of the geometry they enclose** (`npc2
body_4`: geometry 1.176, cached 0.711). A person whose arm swung out was culled
while filling a quarter of the screen. `stopCullingAnimatedParts` now turns
culling off for the five animated people and the player, and leaves it on for
the kits' static props, whose bind pose *is* their pose.

### The one that did not answer: the magician's walk stutter

Could not reproduce it, and measured a lot trying: travel per frame (constant
52.3 mm), heading (0 degrees of rotation), every bone's per-frame displacement
(no outliers — feet peak at 114 mm, normal for a walk), clip switching (Walk
only, for 300 frames), the camera boom (0.58 mm/frame, no reversals), and the
player's own frustum culling (0 draws lost at six camera angles). Three bodies —
road-tripper, mariachi, magician — produce **identical** numbers, so the rig is
not the cause.

Two things were ruled out rather than found: SwiftShader renders ~1 frame per
second (`requestAnimationFrame` gave **1 frame in 1.2 s**), so neither frame
pacing nor anything time-based can be measured headlessly; and `needsUpdate`
shadow measurement came back as 0 draw calls because shadows are off in the
headless preset.

The culling fix above may well be the cure — the magician is a nine-part body
(body, hair, face, hat, top, coat, bottom, feet, hands), each part with its own
cached sphere — but the evidence does not say so, and it should not be claimed.

### Harness notes

- `tmp-found.mjs` holds the veil open with `foundScreen.clearTimers()` before
  screenshotting: the hold is 2.6 s and a SwiftShader screenshot is longer.
- Cycling the selector to reach a specific item needs
  `getCandidates().length + 2` steps, not 6 — a decoy-heavy corner has more.
