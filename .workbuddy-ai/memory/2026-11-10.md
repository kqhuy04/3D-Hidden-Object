# 2026-11-10

## Map picker, and the bone textures that leaked on every swap

**Picker.** `src/ui/MapSelect.ts` + `UIManager` + `Game.loadMap(map)`. Opened from the main menu (`#btn-map`, a pill showing the current map) or the pause menu (`#btn-pause-map`); Escape and BACK return to whichever opened it — never straight to gameplay. Cards come from `MAPS` in `src/data/mapRegistry.ts`, so a new archive adds a card with no UI change. Nine maps: campground + eight kits.

**Swap without reload.** `loadMap` removes the old scene group, frees it, clears collision + target registry, then loads the new world into the same scene. The character is loaded once and kept (`playerReady`); reloading the rig per swap was a visible stall for no benefit. HUD stays mounted, round restarts.

**What does not free itself** (all done by hand in `WorldLoader.unload` / `disposeObject`): geometry, material + its `isTexture` values, the light's 2048² shadow map (`light.dispose()`), the heightfield and collision parts, `renderer.renderLists`, and — the one that was missing — **`Skeleton.dispose()`**.

**The leak.** Every kit ships its props as `SkinnedMesh` (stadium 391, hospital 41) even though nothing animates. Each skeleton allocates a 12×12 bone texture on first draw. That texture belongs to the skeleton, not to the mesh material, so the material sweep missed it: four swaps leaked 851 live textures (576 B each — invisible in frame time, permanent in VRAM). After adding `skeleton.dispose()`, `live` returns to the new map's baseline every time (stadium 392, hospital 50, campground 45).

**Method note.** `renderer.info.memory.textures` is unreliable — it counts every texture *init*, including re-inits after a dispose, so it read 392 for a map whose materials carry zero textures (verified by scanning every material, and by reading the GLB JSON: 0 images, 0 texture refs). The honest measure is patching `gl.createTexture` / `gl.deleteTexture` on the context instance and diffing. Also worth remembering: a leak that is invisible in frame time still needs the counter, and a counter that disagrees with the GL is usually the counter.

**Verified** (headless Chromium, `?debug=1`): 9 cards with the current one marked; click → map changes, `PLAYING`, HUD visible; skatepark body at `(−1, 0.05, 7)` walks `+x`/`−z` unblocked; pause → CHANGE MAP → hospital works; Escape returns to pause; scene graph 3 → 3 children after a swap; no console errors; four swaps return the GL texture count to baseline. `tsc` clean, 57 tests pass, build OK, `dist/` 31 MB.

**Docs:** `docs/MAP_SELECT.md`.

## Round 10 — play-testing all twenty-one maps (the three causes of "the body stops")

**Method.** A seeded random walk, 40 segments × 42 frames per map, 12 % of segments opening with a jump, run with the render loop stopped and the controller stepped by hand — reproducible, and it costs no rendering time. Five scores per segment: distance moved, single-frame drops over 0.5 m, leaving the world, camera behind geometry, NaN. Two sweeps before the fixes, one after.

**Lesson that shaped the round: "stuck" was three different bugs plus one non-bug.**
1. **No camera collision.** `CollisionGeometry.raycastSegment` (slab broad phase → segment-AABB reject per triangle → Möller–Trumbore, no backface culling) + `WorldCollision.cameraDistance` (also samples the heightfield 8×, since terrain is not a collider). `CameraController.pullIn` twice: once for the desired position, once after the lerp, because the lerp can cut a corner the first test never saw. 1.196 ms → 0.797 ms per query on the 1.18 M-triangle map, by rejecting triangles on a segment-AABB and making the second pull conditional on the first having hit.
2. **Crops and bicycles were solid.** `cozy-farm` moved 2.3 m in 28 segments, wedged 0.13 m inside `tomato-crop_2`; `japanese-suburban-city-street` was pinned 10 segments inside `city-bicycle_1`. Both are decoration, not obstacle — added `NON_SOLID_PART_PATTERNS` (`crops|plants|bush|shrub|vine|fern|seedling|sunflower|flower-bed|bicycle|motorbike|scooter`). cozy-farm 2.3 m → 92.5 m; japanese 89.1 m/10 stuck → 117.4 m/0.
3. **The map edge behaved like glue.** `hasGround` false restored the pre-step position and `break`-ed out of the sub-step loop — cancelling the *whole frame's* remaining movement, not just the blocked axis. So walking diagonally into the edge stopped dead instead of sliding. Now it slides per axis, and falls through to the push-out (the allowed axis can still carry the body into a prop). `coral-reef` 7 pinned segments → 1.
4. **Not a bug: pressing forward into a wall.** The first harness counted a segment that moved <5 cm as stuck, which is exactly what walking into a wall does. It now probes 8 directions from wherever the body ended up and reports how many are free; ≥5 means facing a wall, ≤2 means trapped. `space-base`'s "trap" was a hab-module wall — 6/8 free the whole time.

**The camera count was also a harness lie.** The old harness built its own chest point at `feetY + 1.2` and measured to that, while the rig aims at `cameraController.currentLookAt`. A few cm of mismatch makes the segment graze a wall: `space-base` read 8/39 that way and **0/30** with the real look-at point.

**Final sweep, 21 maps:** 840 segments, 31 stopped (all with ≥4 ways out, none trapped), 0 jerks, 0 out-of-world, 0 NaN, 0 camera blocks except one transient on `dia-de-muertos` that does not reproduce on the dedicated test (0/112). Distances 73-127 m. `minY` never below −3.46; water is at −3.75, so no body reaches the surface.

**Harness speed.** Relaunching Chrome costs 50-90 s per map because the character loads behind software-rendered frames, so a 21-map sweep took half an hour. `tmp-wander.mjs` now takes several map ids and swaps them with `selectMap` in one session: ~13 s/map warm. Swapping in place is also the same code path the picker uses. **Do not edit `src/` while a sweep runs** — Vite's HMR reloads the page and the CDP call dies with "Inspected target navigated or closed".

**Two plumbing bugs found on the way.** `__hunt.geometry` was a snapshot, so after `selectMap` a harness probed the *previous* map's collider and got a plausible answer about the wrong map — it is a getter now. And `findSpawn` re-checks the winner's exit count and replaces a hemmed-in spawn with the nearest spot with more ways out (no map needs it today; all 21 resolve to ≥2 exits).

**Verified:** `tsc` clean, 57 tests pass, `npm run build` OK, `dist/` 62 MB. Screenshots of the four old trap spots in `docs/wander/fix-*.png`. Docs: `docs/COLLISION_OVERHAUL.md` §16.

**Real-input pass (`tmp-smoke.mjs`).** The sweep steps the controller directly, so it proves the resolver but not the keyboard → camera → walk wiring. Headless Chromium runs this scene at ~1 fps through SwiftShader, which makes wall-clock waits useless: 1.8 s of holding W is two frames, and a 120 ms Space press lands *between* frames. The harness waits on `requestAnimationFrame` counts instead. Result: Escape → pause → CHANGE MAP → picker; card click loads `japanese-suburban-city-street` at its spawn; W for 8 frames = 3.84 m; Space peaks at 0.55 m and lands; F runs the capture pipeline and the banner reads "NOT IN FRAME — Aim at a target!"; no console errors.

**Useful DOM ids** (guessed wrong first time): picker is `#map-select-screen`, cards are `#map-grid [data-map-id]`, pause button `#btn-pause-map`, capture feedback `#hud-feedback-banner` / `#feedback-text`. Space is edge-latched (`jumpQueued`) so it survives a keyup between frames; F calls `triggerCapture()` directly on keydown.

**Disk:** `tmp-chrome-profile/` is now 603 MB of disposable Chrome cache and still cannot be `rm -rf`'d from bash (`genie-trash failed`) — needs a manual delete or PowerShell. `maps/work/` 807 MB (re-processing source, keep), `public/assets/maps/` 58 MB, `dist/` 62 MB. ## Round 11 — the judder the collision camera introduced

Reported right after round 10: *"walk a little and the screen keeps juddering"*. My own camera-collision code from §16 caused it.

**The mechanism.** `pullIn` snapped a *position* to the wall, `currentPosition.lerp` moved 16.7 % of the way there, then a second `pullIn` snapped the result. Pull, snap, pull, snap — every frame. Measured: **4.50 m of camera movement in one frame** on `cozy-farm`, 0.84 m on `japanese-suburban`, and even 1.2 m of boom wander on `campground` where nothing was in the way (a fixed margin was being subtracted from a distance that had just been measured).

**The fix — the boom becomes state.** `boomDistance` is a length, not a position, rate-limited both ways: retraction at `BOOM_IN_SPEED = 20 m/s`, extension at `BOOM_OUT_RATE = 3.5/s`, with hysteresis on the *target* (not the gap — a gap dead band leaves the camera permanently short). Instant retraction is wrong even though it is what keeps the lens out of geometry: a grazing ray can drop the required boom 7 m between frames, and snapping to it teleports the camera 7.2 m — the same judder, different cause. The ray also reaches `BOOM_MARGIN` past the boom end so "nothing hit" and "hit exactly at the end" differ; without that the camera sat 20 cm short forever.

**Terrain scan quantisation.** `cameraDistance` returned multiples of `length/8` (0.6 m steps on a 5 m boom) because it kept the last clear sample of an 8-point scan. Coarse pass now brackets, seven bisections finish. Samples use the new `terrainHeight` (null off-map) rather than `getGroundHeight`, whose exact-raycast fallback would run ~15× a frame.

**Zooming into a wall shows a hat.** With judder gone, the camera correctly sat 0.5 m behind the chest when backed against a fence → the screen was the back of the head. `MIN_BOOM` 0.45 → 1.1, plus the camera *rises* as the boom squeezes (`pitch = currentPitch + liftSqueeze * 0.6`). The collision ray deliberately uses the **unlifted** pitch: casting along the lifted direction closes a feedback loop (lift clears the obstacle → boom extends → lift drops → obstacle returns) which is a sawtooth.

**Result:** worst single-frame camera move per map is now ≤ **0.455 m** (was 4.50 m), and it is structural — `BOOM_IN_SPEED` bounds it at 0.33 m/frame at 60 fps, so no new map can teleport the camera. Seven maps show 0.08 m (free camera).

**Tests:** new `tests/Camera.test.ts`, 6 cases (reaches configured distance, retracts under 0.5 m/frame, no direction reversal while the obstruction holds, extends gradually, lifts pitch when squeezed, still snaps on `snap()`). 63 total pass.

**Harness:** `tmp-camjitter.mjs` — walks four headings × 90 frames, records per-frame camera step and boom, reports worst step, spike count, boom range and direction reversals. Reversals (not jump count) is the metric for pumping; spike count goes *up* after a fix like this because one 4.5 m jump becomes fifteen 0.3 m frames.

## Round 10 — the pickup bug, and 105 objects that have to be impossible

**The bug the user hit:** walk up to an object, press E or click, nothing happens.

Root cause was one line of geometry, not input. `HiddenItemSystem.findTarget`
measured reach from `cameraPosition`. The camera is a boom camera: it sits
`defaultDistance: 8.0` metres *behind* the body. So `camera→item` was ~8 m even
when the player was standing on the thing, and the 3.2 m reach gate could never
pass. The three gates were all being fed the wrong origin.

Fix: `findTarget(camera, cameraPosition, reachFrom, collision, maxDistance)`.
`reachFrom` is the body at chest height (`REACH_CHEST_HEIGHT = 1.1`), supplied by
`Game.getReachOrigin()`. The camera still does the two jobs it is actually
qualified for — the `offAxis ≤ 0.42` screen-centre test and the occlusion ray.

**Second bug in the same function:** the occlusion tolerance was scaled by the
wrong length. `raycastSegment` returns `t` as a *fraction* of the camera→item
segment, so the 0.35 m tolerance has to multiply by that segment's length, not
the body distance:

```ts
const sightDistance = cameraPosition.distanceTo(_toItem);
if (t !== null && t * sightDistance < sightDistance - 0.35) continue;
```

**Click did nothing at all** — there was no pointer listener for pickup, only
`KeyE`. Added `InputController.onPickupClick`, driven by `pointerdown` /
`pointerup` with a 6-pixel drag threshold so orbiting the camera does not
trigger a pickup, and `pointercancel` to drop a half-started gesture. HUD prompt
now reads `E Pick up or click`.

**The harness was lying.** `tmp-hunt.mjs` overrode the camera to sit 2 m from
each object, so it passed 15/15 while the shipped game could not pick up a
single thing. Lesson: a harness that rewrites the state under test measures the
harness. `tmp-pickable.mjs` replaces it and **never touches the camera** — it
places the body, lets the follow camera settle over real frames, aims by setting
`cameraController.targetYaw` the way a drag would, and asks the game.

Two more harness traps found and fixed:
- `findSpawn` defaults to a **28 m** radius, so it "found" a spawn seven metres
  away and called that standing next to the object — ~47 items falsely failed.
  Now `findSpawn(..., 3)` plus a reject if the answer moved > 2.2 m.
- The two-ring stand-off loop broke only the inner loop, so angle 0 was the only
  angle ever tried. Fixed with a labelled `angleLoop:` break.

**Content: 3 maps / 15 objects → 21 maps / 105 objects.** The user's rule, and
it is the right one: an object must be *impossible* in that place, not merely
unusual. Pizza slice and flat-screen TV are plausible at a campsite, so those
were bad picks. Four of the original fifteen failed that test and were replaced
(camp-pizza → fire hydrant, camp-laptop → parking meter, camp-cone → phone
booth, camp-tv → kitchen sink).

**Method that actually worked for placement.** GLB node names are *mesh part*
names (`body`, `hull`, `slab`), never kit part names (`site-cabin`,
`goods-shed`), so "put it on the picnic table" cannot be resolved by name.
Ask the collision triangles instead: `tmp-spots.mjs` scans for `T` surfaces —
flat tops between knee and chest height with a standable gap — and open ground
`G`. `tmp-place.mjs` then walks candidates *inside the live scene*, scoring each
with `findTarget` from 8 angles × 2 rings, keeping spots ≥ 5 m apart. One
browser launch per map instead of one per attempt. `tmp-apply.mjs` writes the
solver's coordinates back into `src/data/hiddenItems.ts` by id-matched regex.

**Solver bugs, both silent:** first run put four construction-site objects at the
same coordinate (fixed: far candidates first, skip anything within 5 m of a used
spot). Second run still produced six duplicate pairs because KEEP spots were
never pushed into `used` (fixed: record them, and guard the KEEP path).

**Verification:** 21/21 maps report `5/5 pickable`, zero `NOT PICKABLE` lines,
no duplicate placements within 4 m on any map. 46 objects within 10 m of spawn,
14 between 10–20 m, none past 20 m — which is the "khó vừa" difficulty chosen.

## Round 11 — floating pebbles, decoy props, and four new clips

**The pebbles were a flat decal on a curved beach.** Five `pebbles` nodes, each a
single plane of stones ~9 cm thick laid at one Y. Measured against the terrain,
38-41 of every 81 sampled points floated, the worst by 0.49 m. On a shore that
slopes toward the water, a flat decal can only be right along one line.

Fix in `WorldLoader.conformGroundScatter`: bend the mesh onto the terrain before
the collider is built, keeping each vertex's height above the scatter's own base
plane so every stone keeps its thickness. Two guards — name (`pebble|gravel|
shingle|cobble|litter|debris|rubble`) and shape (under 0.5 m thick, over 0.6 m
across) — so a gravel *pile* is left alone. After: no stone floats more than its
own 0.10 m thickness. Costs 31 ms on coral-reef.

**Decoys: every small prop is now clickable.** The prompt used to appear only
next to the five real objects, which made it the answer. `PropTargets` reads the
placement node's children from the scene graph (not the collider — the outline
needs render objects), keeps anything under 2.0 m and over 0.1 m across and not
vegetation. 63 props on campground, 123 on stadium, 161 on coral-reef, 891 on
office. `InteractableSelector` scores candidates by off-axis + 0.02 × distance,
keeps the selection **sticky** across re-sorts, and `Tab` cycles. Three wrong
objects ends the round (`RoundStatus` gained `'lost'`); a rejected prop is struck
off the candidate list so the same mistake cannot be charged twice.

**Outline via OutlinePass**, `EffectComposer` + `RenderPass` + `OutlinePass` +
`OutputPass`, with an explicit 4-sample render target: the composer replaces the
default framebuffer, so MSAA has to be asked for again or every edge in the game
turns jagged. Verified visually on two props.

**Four clips converted** with `tools/convert-fbx-anim.mjs`: stairsUp (1.367 s),
stairsDown (0.400 s — one step, the export really is that short), ladder
(0.767 s), crawl (2.333 s). Note the converter flags stairsUp and ladder as
"airborne, the game moves the root" — that rootLift is the body rising up the
steps, and the controller owns it, so the clips are played **without** it.

**Traversal.** `Gait = flat | up | down | crawl | climb`. Stairs are chosen from
`rise / run` over one frame with a 0.22 s hold, because a staircase reads flat on
every tread. Crawl lowers the capsule to 0.75 m and refuses to stand up under a
ceiling (ray from crawl height to standing height). Ladders are detected from the
map's own `<map>-ladder` props — tall and narrow — and give a climb volume; W/S
move the body, the top hands it to `findSpawn`.

**Harness lesson (again).** Headless SwiftShader renders this scene at 1-2 fps,
so "hold W for a second" simulated *one frame* and the ladder looked broken. The
fix is to step `controller.update(v, yaw, 1/60)` directly in a loop: 90 frames of
simulation, no waiting. Also: a ground profile through a kit staircase shows the
terrain *under* it, flat at ~3.1 m while the prop sits at 3.9 — these kits' stairs
are upper-floor decoration whose lowest tread is 0.7 m up, so they are not
walkable at `stepHeight 0.35`. The gait itself is proven (both `up` and `down`
observed); the staircases are a content problem, not an animation one.

**Verified:** clips all load; crawl 1.75 → 0.75 → 1.75 with the Crawl clip; ladder
0.45 → 2.55 in 90 simulated frames and back down; `StairsDown` fires walking off
a slope; decoys end-to-end (6 candidates in reach, Tab switches, E on a decoy
costs a strike and removes it, E on the hydrant finds it, three strikes → HUNT
OVER). 56 tests, `tsc` clean, build 745 kB.
