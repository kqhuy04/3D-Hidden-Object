NPCs: the kit's own cast, walking about

Every kit ships people — a ranger, an angler, a kid with a torch — and until now
they stood in their modelled pose, which is the loudest thing that says "this is
a model and not a place".

They now walk a short leg, pause, talk to each other, and walk another. Four to
five per map, the ones nearest the spawn, because a farmer idling two hundred
metres away is a cost with no payoff.

Deliberately dull. No pathing graph, no schedules: a leg in a random direction,
validated by six ground samples and a blockage test, rejected if the ground
moves more than 0.3 m under it. That is what keeps them off stairs, out of the
lake and off ledges. When every direction fails they stand still, which is
exactly what the map did before.

The clips come from polyfork (Idle / Walk / Talking, 125 kB total) and are
vendored into public/assets/clips/ rather than fetched from their CDN at play
time: a player on a train should not get a person who never moves.

Three things cost real time here and are worth remembering:

- A merged map renames every bone to keep the file valid: Hips_31, Spine2_18.
  Matching exact names found nobody on twenty maps out of twenty-one, so the
  whole cast stood still. Bone names are compared with the suffix stripped.
- Skinned meshes are baked into the collision soup at their bind pose, so a
  ground query at a character's own feet returns the top of its own legs. Every
  actor measured a 0.37 m offset and every leg was rejected as a cliff. The
  offset is now the median of a ring of samples at 1.2 m, and the height along
  a leg is interpolated from its two ends rather than re-queried per frame.
- A kit character is often several skinned meshes (body, hair, coat), each with
  its own skeleton at the same coordinates. The first run animated five parts
  of one man. Candidates are grouped by where they stand and the rig with the
  most bones wins.

109 tests, tsc clean, build OK, 22/22 maps swept clean.
